 SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

VIVAH is a unified, India-native wedding agency operations platform that takes
a wedding planning agency from the moment a couple makes an enquiry to the moment
the last vendor is paid and the founder reviews the season's profit — in a single
system with zero data loss, zero payment confusion, and real-time financial
visibility.

The name VIVAH (विवाह) is the Sanskrit/Hindi word for wedding/marriage. It is
familiar, culturally anchored, and immediately communicates the domain.

VIVAH is not a couple-facing wedding planning app. It is not a vendor marketplace.
It is not a guest management tool. It is the operational backbone for the AGENCY
— the team of professional wedding planners who orchestrate the entire event.

The core thesis of VIVAH is:

  "A wedding planning agency using VIVAH can manage twice as many weddings with
   zero payments lost, zero vendors underpaid, zero tasks missed, and real-time
   visibility into every rupee earned and spent — without adding more staff."

This is built for an agency exactly like The Royal Knot (Jaipur/Udaipur), doing
25 luxury weddings per season with 18+ vendors per wedding and budgets in crores.
But the system scales to any agency doing 5 to 100 weddings per season.

 SECTION 2: THE PROBLEM — WHAT IS ACTUALLY BROKEN
================================================================================

To understand why VIVAH needs to exist, you need to understand what a wedding
planning agency actually does in the course of one wedding season, and where
exactly the operational chaos lives today.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

2.1 THE SCALE OF ONE WEDDING

A typical luxury Indian wedding is not a single event. It is a 2 to 5 day,
multi-function production. The functions typically include:

  Day 1: Mehendi ceremony (150–300 guests, daytime, informal)
  Day 2: Sangeet night (300–500 guests, evening, performance-heavy)
  Day 3: Wedding ceremony + Reception (400–1000 guests, the main event)

Each function has its own:
  - Venue or venue zone
  - Guest list (not all guests attend all functions)
  - Decor theme
  - Food and catering requirement
  - Entertainment lineup
  - Photography and video crew
  - Schedule and timeline
  - Vendor set

A single wedding therefore involves:
  → 18 to 22 different vendor categories
  → 400+ guests (some weddings 1000+)
  → 3 to 5 days of live execution
  → ₹20 lakh to ₹5 crore total budget
  → 25 to 40 task items per wedding across planning and execution
  → 3 to 5 payment milestones per vendor
  → Multiple change orders after the event (extra guests, extended DJ hours,
     additional flower arrangements, fuel overruns, etc.)

Now multiply this by 25 weddings in a season. That is the world the agency
operates in.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

2.2 THE 6 BROKEN SCENARIOS (REAL PAIN POINTS)

BROKEN SCENARIO 1: THE LOST ENQUIRY
  A couple calls from Instagram after seeing a wedding reel. Nobody picks up.
  They WhatsApp. The message gets buried under 47 other messages. Three days
  later someone sees it. The couple has already hired a competitor.

  Current state: Enquiries arrive via Instagram DMs, WhatsApp, phone calls,
  website contact forms, and wedding marketplace referrals. No single inbox.
  No tracking. No follow-up reminder. A typical agency loses 20–30% of warm
  leads simply by not responding in time.

BROKEN SCENARIO 2: THE VENDOR QUOTE NIGHTMARE
  The planner needs a caterer for 400 guests. They ask 3 caterers for quotes.
  Each caterer responds with a different PDF over WhatsApp 2–3 days later.
  One quotes per plate. One quotes a package. One includes bar service. One
  does not. The planner opens three PDFs side by side, creates an Excel sheet
  manually, types in the numbers, tries to normalize everything to a per-plate
  rate, and shares a summary with the couple.

  This process takes 4–6 hours per vendor category. There are 18 vendor
  categories. The planner does this for every wedding. For 25 weddings a season,
  this is hundreds of hours of pure admin work that creates zero value.

BROKEN SCENARIO 3: THE REWRITTEN CHECKLIST
  Every time a new wedding is confirmed, the planner opens last season's paper
  diary, finds the old checklist, and rewrites it with new names and dates. Some
  tasks get forgotten. Venue booking is remembered; earthing kit for the sound
  system is not. By the time the wedding day arrives, someone realizes three tasks
  were never completed.

  Current state: Checklists exist in paper diaries, WhatsApp notes, or personal
  Google Docs. They are never standardized. They are rewritten from scratch or
  copied imperfectly for each wedding. Ownership of tasks is unclear. Deadlines
  are not tracked.

BROKEN SCENARIO 4: THE DAY-OF CHAOS
  It is the wedding day. The DJ was supposed to arrive at 9 AM for sound check.
  It is 9:45 AM. Nobody knows where he is. The planner is on the phone with the
  groom's family about the baraat timing. The assistant is managing the caterer's
  setup. The florist needs an extra generator. The bride's car is stuck in traffic.
  The planner has no single view of what is happening where, who has arrived, who
  is late, and who needs to make a decision in the next 10 minutes.

  Current state: Day-of coordination happens via a WhatsApp group with 30+
  people, voice calls, and shouting across a venue. Vendors confirm arrival
  verbally and nobody records it. Problems surface only when they are already
  crises.

BROKEN SCENARIO 5: THE PAYMENT CHAOS
  The wedding is over. 15 vendors were paid advances. 60 extra guests showed up
  at the banquet. The DJ was extended by 2 hours. An extra flower arch was added
  last minute. The planner now has to:
    → Calculate what each vendor is owed (total agreed minus advance already paid)
    → Add extras for each vendor (extra plates, extra hours, extra items)
    → Bill the couple for all the extras (and hope they don't dispute it)
    → Make sure no vendor gets paid twice
    → Make sure no vendor is forgotten
    → Record all of this somewhere

  Current state: This is done from memory, WhatsApp message history, and
  notebooks. Vendors get paid wrong amounts. Extras go unbilled. Couples
  dispute charges because there is no documented record. The agency loses
  money on every wedding due to reconciliation errors.

BROKEN SCENARIO 6: THE INVISIBLE PROFIT
  The founder sits down at the end of the season and asks: which of the 25
  weddings made money, and which lost money? The answer is: nobody knows. The
  agency knows total revenue from couples. They roughly know total vendor costs.
  But per-wedding profitability, accounting for team hours, travel costs, extras,
  and reconciliation errors, has never been calculated accurately. The founder
  is running a multi-crore business completely blind.

  Current state: Monthly Tally entries or basic Excel sheets. No per-wedding
  P&L. No team hour cost allocation. No season dashboard. No cash flow forecast.
  Profit is discovered — if at all — months after the season ends.

 SECTION 3: MARKET RESEARCH — WHAT EXISTS TODAY

We researched 25+ tools across India, the US, EU, UK, and Australia. Here is

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

3.1 INDIA — THE LOCAL MARKET

India has 1,544 active wedding-tech companies as of 2026, with only $70 million
in total sector funding across all of them. This is shockingly low for a $139
billion market. The reason: almost all funding went to MARKETPLACES (vendor
discovery for couples) and not to AGENCY OPERATIONS SOFTWARE.

India-specific tools audited:

  WEDDINGKART
  → WhatsApp-native guest management, RSVP automation, multi-wedding dashboards
  → Priced per-wedding at ₹4,999+
  → Strong on guest communication via WhatsApp
  → Completely absent: vendor operations, payment reconciliation, P&L

  WEDD.AI
  → AI-powered WhatsApp RSVP automation
  → Strongest feature: handles 70–80% of repetitive guest questions via bot
  → Useful as one component; useless as an agency ops tool

  PLAN A WEDDING
  → Claims to be "India's First Wedding Industry Operating System"
  → Has a dual-app ecosystem: couple-facing + enterprise CRM for planners
  → Covers leads, tasks, vendor lists, milestones across 350+ cities
  → Gaps: No vendor quote comparison, no per-wedding P&L, no live day-of
    vendor check-in, no payment reconciliation with extras

  SHAADI SCHEDULER
  → AI-generated checklists per event type
  → Couple-facing tool, not planner-facing
  → Zero vendor operations or financial tracking

  WEDMEGOOD / WEDDINGWIRE INDIA
  → Pure marketplaces for vendor discovery
  → Not operational tools at all
  → Help couples FIND vendors; do nothing for planner workflow

  CONCLUSION ON INDIA:
  Every Indian tool falls into one of two buckets: marketplace for couples,
  or guest management for families. ZERO tools build the agency's operational
  backbone: vendor quoting, task management, payment lifecycle, day-of
  execution, and per-wedding financial reporting.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

3.2 GLOBAL — US / EU / UK / AUSTRALIA

  HONEYBOOK (US) — $16–79/month
  → Best-in-class for inquiry to contract to client payment
  → Automated follow-up sequences, digital proposals, e-signatures,
    installment payment plans, auto-reminders, revenue summary
  → Critical gap: It tracks CLIENTS, not VENDORS. No vendor roster,
    no vendor payment tracking, no vendor quote comparison, no day-of
    timeline, no guest lists, no floor plans
  → Designed exclusively for US market (USD, US tax, US contract formats)

  AISLE PLANNER (US) — $49.99–189.99/month
  → Gold standard for planning and timelines
  → AI-powered timeline builder, vendor coordination portal, itemized budget
    tracking, design boards, checklists, task management
  → Critical gap: No live day-of vendor check-in, no vendor quote comparison,
    no per-wedding P&L, no financial season dashboard
  → Vendor info does not carry over between weddings efficiently
  → US-centric (no Indian multi-day structure, no Indian payment customs)

  HARPSEN (US/UK)
  → Strong on execution: run-of-show views, vendor responsibilities, team
    assignments, proposal-to-execution in one place
  → Relatively new; depth of vendor payment reconciliation unclear

  TRUE CLIENT PRO (US)
  → Has vendor quote comparison and vendor payment tracking (original quote
    vs. amount paid vs. amount remaining per vendor)
  → The closest globally to solving the vendor payment problem
  → No day-of live vendor check-in, no season P&L, no India features

  BRIDAL INTEL (US)
  → AI-powered structured vendor quote comparison dashboard
  → The best-in-class for comparing vendor quotes side by side
  → Standalone vendor sourcing tool only — no project management, no payments,
    no execution features

  WEDINEST (EU)
  → CRM for planners: reusable vendor directory across weddings, paid/pending
    amounts per vendor at a glance, tasks and timelines per wedding
  → No quote comparison, no day-of operations, no season-level analytics

  PLANNING POD (US) — $59.99–129/month
  → Most comprehensive generic event management: CRM, budgets, logistics,
    floor planning, BEO generation, day-of deliverables
  → Not wedding-specific, not India-specific

  CONCLUSION ON GLOBAL:
  US/EU wedding planners use a STACK of 2–4 tools: HoneyBook for client
  onboarding + payments, Aisle Planner for planning + timelines, True Client
  Pro or spreadsheets for vendor tracking, QuickBooks for accounting.
  This stack costs $100–320/month and still leaves major gaps: no vendor
  quote comparison, no live day-of vendor check-in with alerts, no per-wedding
  P&L with vendor costs, no Indian multi-day wedding structure.

 SECTION 4: REFERENCE SYSTEMS — WHAT WE BORROWED FROM
================================================================================

We are not building from zero. We have taken the best patterns from existing
tools and either directly referenced them or adapted them for Indian context.
Here is exactly what we took from whom and why:

  FROM HONEYBOOK:
  → Inquiry-to-proposal-to-contract-to-payment in ONE linear flow
  → Automated follow-up reminder logic when an enquiry hasn't been responded to
  → Installment payment schedule with milestone tracking
  → The client portal concept (shared view for couple)
  Why we borrowed this: HoneyBook's client onboarding flow is the best in the
  world. We replicate its simplicity but adapt it for Indian payment customs
  (INR, advance/balance structure, WhatsApp as primary channel).

  FROM AISLE PLANNER:
  → Task checklist template concept (pre-built checklist auto-loaded on booking)
  → Vendor coordination: vendors linked to a specific wedding with their
    documents, schedules, and responsibilities
  → Timeline builder: event-by-event schedule with time slots and responsibilities
  Why we borrowed this: The template-first approach to planning is the right
  paradigm. Instead of starting from zero for each wedding, the planner starts
  from a complete Indian wedding template and modifies it.

  FROM BRIDAL INTEL:
  → The structured vendor quote comparison table (normalize competing quotes
    to the same format for apples-to-apples comparison)
  Why we borrowed this: This is the single most powerful concept for vendor
  management. We adapt it for the Indian market by including per-plate pricing,
  extra-guest rate, inclusion/exclusion breakdown, and payment terms comparison.

  FROM TRUE CLIENT PRO:
  → Vendor payment ledger: original quote, advance paid, balance remaining,
    extras added, final amount due — per vendor, per wedding
  Why we borrowed this: The closest existing tool to solving vendor payment
  reconciliation. We extend it to handle Indian vendor payment customs (token →
  advance → pre-event → post-event) and the extras/change-order workflow.

  FROM WEDINEST:
  → Persistent vendor directory that carries performance history across weddings
  → Season-level view of all weddings simultaneously
  Why we borrowed this: The reusable vendor directory is critical. A planner
  should not re-enter Rajesh Caterers' details for every wedding. They work with
  the same 30–50 vendors repeatedly. Ratings from past weddings should inform
  future selections.

  FOR DESIGN AND MOOD BOARDS:
  We are NOT building a mood board or design tool. This problem is solved.
  We will use direct API integration or embed links to:
  → Pinterest boards (couples already use this to communicate their vision)
  → Canva designs (planners already use this for mood boards and floor plans)
  → Google Drive links (for storing signed contracts and final design files)

  Each wedding record in VIVAH will have a "Design References" section where
  the planner attaches Pinterest links, Canva links, or uploads a PDF/image.
  We are not reinventing the mood board. We are contextualizing it within the
  wedding record so it is never lost in a WhatsApp thread.

  Why this is the right call: Building a mood board tool from scratch in 48 hours
  is both unnecessary and wasteful. Pinterest and Canva are already best-in-class.
  The gap is not the design tool itself; it is the fact that the design reference
  lives in a different place from the vendor list, the budget, and the timeline.
  VIVAH solves the context problem, not the design tool problem.

 SECTION 5: THE GAP — WHAT NOBODY HAS BUILT
================================================================================

After researching every major tool globally and locally, here is a precise map
of what has NEVER been built in a single unified system:

  GAP 1: VENDOR QUOTE COMPARISON INTEGRATED INTO A PROJECT
  No tool lets a planner send an RFQ to multiple vendors within the same wedding
  project, receive structured (not PDF) quotes, compare them side by side with
  normalized pricing, select a winner, auto-close the others, and have the
  selected vendor's payment schedule automatically flow into the wedding's
  financial tracker — all in one action, from within the wedding record.
  Currently this is 4 to 6 hours of manual work per vendor category.

  GAP 2: LIVE DAY-OF COMMAND CENTER WITH VENDOR CHECK-IN
  No tool anywhere in the world provides a live, real-time, minute-by-minute
  schedule visible to all team members simultaneously where:
    → Each vendor can be marked as "Arrived" with a timestamp
    → The system automatically flags when a vendor is overdue (hasn't arrived
       by their scheduled time)
    → The planner gets a push/in-app alert
    → Team members see updates in real time
    → The schedule can be adjusted live (push all events by 30 minutes)
    → Different team members have role-based views

  GAP 3: VENDOR PAYMENT RECONCILIATION WITH CHANGE ORDERS
  No tool handles the post-wedding financial close:
    → What did we agree to pay each vendor?
    → What advance have we already paid?
    → What extras were added (extra plates, extra hours, extra items)?
    → What does the vendor's final invoice say vs. what we agreed?
    → What do we now owe each vendor?
    → What do we bill the couple for extras?
    → Has this vendor already been paid for any portion of this extra?
  This end-to-end reconciliation flow is manually done in every agency today.

  GAP 4: PER-WEDDING P&L IN REAL TIME
  No tool calculates profit per wedding as:
    Revenue (agency fee + commissions) minus Costs (vendor payments + team
    hours + travel + miscellaneous) = Net profit per wedding
  With the ability to view this for all weddings in a season simultaneously,
  ranked by profitability, with outstanding payments factored in.

  GAP 5: INDIA-NATIVE MULTI-FUNCTION WEDDING DATA MODEL
  Every global tool models a wedding as a single event on a single date.
  Indian weddings are 2–5 distinct functions (Mehendi, Sangeet, Wedding,
  Reception) over multiple days, each with its own vendors, guests, timeline,
  and budget. No existing tool has this data model. This is a structural moat
  that cannot be retrofitted onto Western-designed tools.

  GAP 6: WHATSAPP-FIRST COMMUNICATION IN AN OPERATIONS CONTEXT
  Indian planners, couples, and vendors communicate primarily on WhatsApp.
  Western tools are email-first. The result is that the operational system
  and the actual communication channel are always different applications,
  leading to information loss when decisions made on WhatsApp are never
  recorded in the system.

 SECTION 6: PROPOSED SOLUTION — WHAT VIVAH IS
================================================================================

VIVAH is a web-based, mobile-responsive, India-native wedding agency operations
platform built around six core modules that together form a complete operational
system from enquiry to season review.

The platform is designed for two types of users within a wedding agency:

  THE PLANNER: Manages individual weddings. Books vendors. Tracks tasks.
  Coordinates day-of execution. Handles vendor payments.

  THE FOUNDER / DIRECTOR: Views all weddings simultaneously. Monitors cash
  flow. Reviews per-wedding profitability. Sees outstanding payments.
  Makes strategic decisions based on real data.

VIVAH's design philosophy:

  1. ZERO DATA LOSS: Every enquiry, quote, decision, payment, and event is
     recorded in the system. Nothing lives in a WhatsApp thread or paper diary.

  2. STRUCTURED OVER FREE-FORM: Instead of text fields and open notes, VIVAH
     uses structured data. A vendor quote is not a PDF; it is a form with
     specific fields. A payment is not a WhatsApp message; it is a ledger entry.
     This makes comparison, calculation, and reporting possible.

  3. AUTOMATION WHERE IT SAVES TIME: When a wedding is confirmed, the task
     checklist is auto-generated. When a vendor is selected from quotes, the
     others are auto-closed. When a payment milestone arrives, the system flags
     it. The planner should not have to remember — the system remembers.

  4. FINANCIAL ACCURACY AS NON-NEGOTIABLE: Every rupee that goes in and out
     is tracked at the wedding level, at the vendor level, and at the season
     level. The system cannot produce a wrong balance. It is double-entry
     aware: you cannot pay a vendor more than what is owed. You cannot mark a
     payment as complete without recording the amount and date.

  5. INDIA FIRST: Multi-function wedding data model. Indian payment customs
     (token → advance → milestone → final). INR everywhere. WhatsApp as the
     communication layer. Indian vendor categories (Pandit, Dhol, Mehendi
     artist, Rangoli artist, Trousseau packing, etc.). Jain menu as a dietary
     option. Multi-day event structure.

 SECTION 8: SYSTEM MODULES — DETAILED DESCRIPTION
================================================================================

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
MODULE 1: ENQUIRY & COUPLE MANAGEMENT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

PURPOSE:
Ensure that no enquiry is ever lost, and that every couple's information,
preferences, and decisions are stored in one place from the first contact.

HOW IT WORKS:

  ENQUIRY CAPTURE
  The planner (or an embedded web form that couples fill out) captures:
    → Bride & Groom names
    → Contact number and email
    → Source of enquiry (Instagram, WhatsApp, WedMeGood, referral, website)
    → Approximate wedding date or date range
    → Preferred location (Jaipur, Udaipur, home city, destination)
    → Guest count estimate
    → Budget range (₹ bracket selection)
    → Type of wedding (Hindu, Muslim, Christian, Sikh, Fusion, Civil)
    → Functions requested (Mehendi, Haldi, Sangeet, Wedding, Reception —
       each as a toggle)
    → Vibe/theme preferences (text + option to paste a Pinterest URL)
    → Package interest (Full Service or Day-Of Coordination)
    → Special notes or non-negotiables

  ENQUIRY PIPELINE
  Every enquiry moves through a visual kanban-style pipeline:
    ENQUIRY → CONSULTATION SCHEDULED → PROPOSAL SENT → NEGOTIATION →
    BOOKED (deposit paid) → PLANNING IN PROGRESS → EXECUTION →
    COMPLETED → CLOSED (lost / cancelled)

  At each stage:
    → The planner assigned to the enquiry is shown
    → The date it entered that stage is recorded
    → A follow-up reminder can be set (e.g., "Call back in 3 days if
       no response to proposal")
    → Notes can be added at any stage

  SERVICE AGREEMENT
  Once a couple confirms and pays the booking deposit:
    → The system generates a service agreement from a standard template
      populated with the couple's details, chosen package, total budget,
      payment schedule, and key terms
    → The planner downloads this as a PDF, sends via WhatsApp/email
    → After signing, the signed PDF is uploaded back and attached to the
       couple's record permanently
    → The booking deposit is recorded in the payment engine simultaneously

  COUPLE PROFILE (The Master Record)
  Every couple has a permanent profile in the system containing:
    → Personal details
    → All functions with dates, venues, and estimated guest counts
    → Linked wedding project (tasks, vendors, timeline)
    → Design references (Pinterest URLs, Canva links, uploaded images)
    → All payments (to/from)
    → All communications log
    → Signed agreement attachment
    → Notes from each planner interaction

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
MODULE 2: WEDDING PROJECT & TASK BOARD
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

PURPOSE:
Replace the paper diary. Auto-generate a complete, wedding-specific task plan
the moment a wedding is confirmed, and track every task to completion.

HOW IT WORKS:

================================================================================
 SECTION 9: FINANCIAL ENGINE — THE CORE PROMISE
================================================================================

The financial engine is not just a feature. It is the architectural spine of
the entire platform. Every other module feeds data into it. Here is how we
ensure zero financial errors:

PRINCIPLE 1: IMMUTABLE PAYMENT RECORDS
Every payment recorded — whether from a couple or to a vendor — is an
immutable ledger entry. It cannot be deleted. It can only be corrected by
creating a reversal entry. This creates an audit trail for every rupee.

PRINCIPLE 2: CALCULATED FIELDS, NEVER MANUAL TOTALS
No total amount is ever manually entered. Every total is computed by the system:
  → Outstanding from couple = Total budget + Extras - Total collected
  → Outstanding to vendor = Agreed total + Extras - Total paid
  → Net profit = (Agency fee + commissions) - (Team costs + Travel)
  → Cash position = Total collected from couples - Total paid to vendors

This means there is no way to have a wrong total from a typo.

PRINCIPLE 3: CHANGE ORDERS ARE TRACEABLE
Every extra charge (beyond the original quote) is recorded as a change order
with: date added, who added it, which vendor it relates to, and the amount.
Change orders are visible in both the vendor's expense ledger and the couple's
income ledger. The couple can see exactly what they are being charged and why.

PRINCIPLE 4: STATUS-LOCKED PAYMENTS
A payment tranche can only be in one of these states: UPCOMING / DUE /
OVERDUE / PAID. It cannot be in PAID and UPCOMING simultaneously. Once
PAID with a reference number, it is locked. Any attempt to re-pay triggers
a warning requiring explicit override with a reason.

PRINCIPLE 5: REAL-TIME, NOT END-OF-MONTH
The financial position updates the moment any payment is recorded. The founder
does not wait for a monthly report. The dashboard shows the current state of
every rupee at any time of day.

 SECTION 10: DATA RELATIONSHIPS — HOW EVERYTHING CONNECTS
================================================================================

The following entities exist in VIVAH and relate to each other as described:

  AGENCY (1)
    → Has many PLANNERS (users)
    → Has many WEDDINGS per season
    → Maintains one VENDOR DIRECTORY
    → Has a SEASON dashboard

  COUPLE (1 couple = 1 wedding record)
    → Has multiple FUNCTIONS (Mehendi, Sangeet, Wedding, Reception)
    → Has one INCOME LEDGER (payments from couple to agency)
    → Has one TASK LIST (auto-generated on booking)
    → Has one set of DESIGN REFERENCES (links and uploads)
    → Is linked to one SERVICE AGREEMENT document

  FUNCTION (part of a wedding)
    → Has a date, venue, guest count
    → Has a set of VENDORS assigned (one per category)
    → Has a DAY-OF SCHEDULE (timeline of events)
    → Has a BUDGET allocation

  VENDOR (in the master directory)
    → Belongs to one or more CATEGORIES
    → Has a PERFORMANCE HISTORY (ratings across past weddings)
    → Can receive multiple RFQs for different weddings
    → When selected for a wedding, is linked to that WEDDING + FUNCTION

  RFQ (Request for Quote)
    → Belongs to one WEDDING + FUNCTION + CATEGORY
    → Has one or more VENDOR QUOTES submitted against it
    → One VENDOR QUOTE is marked as SELECTED (winner)
    → All other VENDOR QUOTES are marked as NOT SELECTED (auto-closed)
    → The SELECTED VENDOR QUOTE creates a VENDOR PAYMENT SCHEDULE

  VENDOR PAYMENT SCHEDULE
    → Belongs to one VENDOR + one WEDDING
    → Has multiple PAYMENT TRANCHES (advance, pre-event, final)
    → Each TRANCHE can have CHANGE ORDERS added to it
    → Each TRANCHE has a PAYMENT RECORD when paid

  PAYMENT RECORD (for both income and expense)
    → Belongs to either the INCOME LEDGER or EXPENSE LEDGER
    → Has: amount, date, method, reference, who recorded it
    → Is IMMUTABLE once created (reversal only)

  TASK
    → Belongs to one WEDDING
    → Optionally linked to one FUNCTION
    → Assigned to one PLANNER
    → Optionally linked to one VENDOR and/or one PAYMENT TRANCHE

  TIMELINE EVENT (Day-Of Schedule)
    → Belongs to one WEDDING + one FUNCTION
    → Has: scheduled time, event name, vendor, planner responsible
    → Has a CHECK-IN RECORD when vendor marks as arrived
    → Has a PROBLEM FLAG if raised during execution

 SECTION 11: DESIGN APPROACH — HOW WE HANDLE UI/UX
================================================================================

We are not building a design tool. We are referencing and integrating with
tools that already do this perfectly.

WHAT WE USE FROM EXISTING TOOLS:

  MOOD BOARDS & DESIGN REFERENCES:
    → Pinterest: Each couple's wedding record has a field for their
      Pinterest board URL. The planner adds this during the consultation.
      The URL is stored and displayed as a clickable thumbnail in the couple's
      profile. No mood board is rebuilt inside VIVAH.

    → Canva: Floor plans, seating charts, and invitation designs created by
      the planner in Canva are shared as a Canva view link and stored in
      the wedding record under "Design Files."

    → Google Drive: For large files (full photo albums, signed contracts,
      vendor invoices), Google Drive links are stored in the relevant
      record (couple profile or vendor payment record).

  WHY THIS IS THE RIGHT DECISION:
    Building a design tool in 48 hours is impossible to do well, and it is
    unnecessary. Planners already use Pinterest and Canva daily. They know
    these tools. VIVAH's job is to contextualize these references within the
    operational record, not to replace them. A mood board link in the wedding
    record is infinitely more useful than a broken half-baked design tool.

  VIVAH'S OWN UI DESIGN APPROACH:
    The UI of VIVAH follows a dashboard-first, data-dense, operations-oriented
    design language. Not the light, pretty, couple-facing aesthetic of
    WeddingWire. Think closer to Linear, Notion, or Jira — clean, structured,
    information-rich, and task-focused. Because this is a tool for planners
    who are professionals managing crores of rupees, not couples dreaming
    about their wedding.

    Color system:
      → Primary: Deep Burgundy (#6B2737) — wedding-appropriate, professional
      → Secondary: Warm Gold (#B8860B) — luxury context
      → Background: Off-white (#FAFAF8) — easy on the eyes for long sessions
      → Alerts: Standard Red / Amber / Green for status indicators
      → Data tables: Clean, bordered, high-contrast for readability

    Typography: Inter (clean, professional, highly legible in dense tables)

    Component library: Shadcn/UI or Chakra UI for React — pre-built,
    accessible, production-quality components so we are not designing from
    scratch. We assemble, not design.

    Responsive: Mobile-first for the Day-Of Command Center (planners are on
    phones during the event). Desktop-optimized for the Founder Dashboard
    and planning modules (planners sit at desks for vendor management and
    financial work).

 SECTION 12: WHY THIS APPROACH — JUSTIFICATION OF DECISIONS
================================================================================

WHY WEB APP, NOT MOBILE APP?
  → A web app (responsive) works on any device without an app store submission.
  → The Day-Of module is mobile-responsive — it works perfectly on a phone
    without being a native app.
  → In 48 hours, a native app is impossible to build and demo properly.
  → React/Next.js with responsive CSS gives us mobile functionality where
    needed (Day-Of) and desktop richness where needed (dashboards).

WHY NOT BUILD AI FEATURES?
  → AI features add complexity without demonstrating the core value.
  → The core value of VIVAH is STRUCTURED DATA and AUTOMATED CALCULATION,
    not AI. The system's intelligence comes from enforcing structure, not
    from machine learning.
  → AI vendor matching or AI timeline generation are features for V2. In
    a hackathon, they would be fake demos that distract from real functionality.

WHY POSTGRES (RELATIONAL DATABASE)?
  → Wedding data is HIGHLY relational: weddings have functions, functions
    have vendors, vendors have payment schedules, payment schedules have
    tranches, tranches have payments and change orders.
  → A relational model with foreign keys and constraints ENFORCES data
    integrity at the database level. A NoSQL approach would require this
    to be enforced in application code — which is error-prone and unsuitable
    for a financial system.
  → PostgreSQL has native support for transactions, which are essential
    for the payment ledger (atomic operations: record payment + update
    balance in a single transaction that either fully completes or fully
    rolls back).

WHY NOT JUST USE EXISTING TOOLS (HONEYBOOK + AISLE PLANNER)?
  → HoneyBook: Tracks clients, not vendors. USD. No Indian payment customs.
    No multi-day Indian wedding structure. No vendor quote comparison.
    No day-of operations. No per-wedding P&L.
  → Aisle Planner: US-centric. No Indian functions. No vendor payment
    reconciliation. No live day-of check-in. No financial reporting.
  → Stacking them: $120–270/month. Data still in two places. No integration
    between them. The planner still manually transfers data.
  → VIVAH: ₹5,000–8,000/month. One system. One data model. All connected.
    India-first. Built for this exact use case.

WHY PER-WEDDING PROFITABILITY MATTERS:
  The founder of The Royal Knot is doing 25 weddings at several crores each.
  Without per-wedding P&L, they are running blind. A ₹1 crore wedding that
  required 400 team hours and 6 site visits might make less profit than a
  ₹40 lakh wedding that ran smoothly in 150 hours. Without data, every
  wedding feels like revenue. With VIVAH, every wedding shows its true cost.

 SECTION 13: WHAT WE ARE NOT BUILDING
================================================================================

These are explicitly out of scope for the hackathon prototype. They are
acknowledged, referenced, and left for V2:

  ❌ FULL GUEST MANAGEMENT SYSTEM
     We capture guest count per function. We do not build RSVP tracking,
     digital invitations, individual guest records, seating arrangements,
     or dietary tracking per guest. WeddingKart and Wedd.ai do this. In
     VIVAH we reference their integration as a V2 feature.

  ❌ PAYMENT GATEWAY INTEGRATION
     Payments are marked as paid manually with a reference number. No
     Razorpay / Stripe / PayU integration. The financial calculation engine
     is real; the payment capture is manual.

  ❌ E-SIGNATURE (FUNCTIONAL)
     Service agreements are generated as PDFs and the signed version is
     uploaded back. A real DocuSign/DigiSigner integration is referenced
     in the UI but not wired up in the prototype.

  ❌ GST AND TDS COMPUTATION
     We track amounts and note whether a vendor is GST-registered. We do
     not compute GST per transaction or handle TDS deductions automatically.
     These are noted in the payment record for the accountant.

  ❌ WHATSAPP API INTEGRATION
     Communications are logged manually. Real WhatsApp Business API
     integration (auto-sending payment reminders, RFQ summaries, vendor
     confirmations via WhatsApp) is a V2 feature.

  ❌ VENDOR SELF-SERVICE PORTAL
     Vendors cannot log in to see their schedule or submit quotes directly.
     All vendor data is entered by the planner. V2 adds a vendor portal.

  ❌ MOBILE NATIVE APP
     Responsive web only. Native iOS/Android app is V2.

  ❌ ACCOUNTING INTEGRATION
     No Tally, Zoho Books, or QuickBooks sync. VIVAH's financial data can
     be exported as CSV for import into accounting software. Full API
     integration is V2.

  ❌ AI / ML FEATURES
     No AI vendor matching, no AI timeline generation, no predictive
     analytics. Pure deterministic logic only.

================================================================================

TEAM SIZE: 4 members
DURATION: 48 hours

The work is divided by MODULE OWNERSHIP, not by frontend/backend split.
Each person owns a full vertical slice of their modules: database schema,
API, and UI. This avoids coordination overhead and allows parallel development.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PERSON 1: FOUNDATION + ENQUIRY + COUPLE MANAGEMENT
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
OWNS: Project setup, database schema, authentication, Module 1

Responsibilities:
  → Project repository setup (Next.js, Tailwind, Shadcn/UI, Prisma + Postgres)
  → Database schema design and migration for ALL entities (this person
    defines the data model the entire team uses — most critical early task)
  → Authentication system (login, roles: Founder / Lead Planner / Assistant)
  → Module 1: Enquiry capture form
  → Module 1: Enquiry pipeline (kanban-style status board)
  → Module 1: Couple profile page
  → Module 1: Design references section (Pinterest/Canva link storage)
  → Module 1: Service agreement upload
  → Shared navigation, layout, and design system setup for all other members

WHY THIS PERSON:
  The foundation and data model work requires the strongest backend/architecture
  thinking. All other modules depend on the schema this person defines.
  Getting it wrong here cascades into every other module. This person also
  sets up the shared UI component library so everyone else's screens look
  consistent without coordination overhead.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PERSON 2: VENDOR DIRECTORY + QUOTE COMPARISON (Module 3)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
OWNS: Module 3 (Vendor Directory and Quote Comparison)

Responsibilities:
  → Vendor master directory: create, view, search, filter, rate vendors
  → Vendor category system (all Indian wedding categories pre-loaded)
  → RFQ creation for a wedding + vendor category
  → Structured quote entry form (category-specific fields)
  → Side-by-side quote comparison table (the killer feature)
  → Vendor selection flow: select winner, auto-close others
  → On selection: auto-create vendor payment schedule (feeds into Module 4)
  → On selection: auto-link vendor to wedding timeline (feeds into Module 5)
  → Vendor performance history: past ratings, weddings worked, issues

WHY THIS PERSON:
  The quote comparison module is the most technically complex UI feature in
  the system. It requires dynamic form generation (different fields per
  category), normalization logic (computing effective per-plate price from
  different quote structures), and the selection cascade (one action triggering
  multiple downstream updates). This is a full module by itself.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PERSON 3: PAYMENT ENGINE + TASK BOARD (Modules 4 + 2)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
OWNS: Module 4 (Payment Engine) + Module 2 (Task Board)

Responsibilities:
  → Income ledger: couple payment milestones, record payment, calculate
    outstanding, overdue alerts
  → Expense ledger: vendor payment tranches, record payment, double-payment
    prevention, validate against agreed amount
  → Change orders: add extras, link to vendor, recalculate couple final bill
  → Per-wedding financial summary (auto-calculated, real-time)
  → Task checklist template: pre-build the Indian wedding master template
  → Auto-generation of tasks on wedding confirmation (from template)
  → Task board: kanban view, list view, my tasks view
  → Task status management: assign, update, mark done
  → Overdue task alerts

WHY THIS PERSON:
  Module 4 (Payment Engine) is the most mathematically sensitive part of
  the system and demands the most careful backend logic. Immutable records,
  transaction-safe payment recording, double-payment prevention, and cascade
  recalculation on change orders all need robust implementation. Pairing this
  with Module 2 (which is simpler — template + task board) balances the
  workload while keeping financial logic owned by one person who can focus
  on getting it right.

PERSON 4: DAY-OF COMMAND CENTER + FOUNDER DASHBOARD (Modules 5 + 6)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
